Check Broker License: FCA, CySEC, ASIC & SEC Step by Step

The broker's website shows an FCA registration number. You Google it — it exists. But the number belongs to a completely different company, and the 'broker' is a clone. Licence verification is the single most-skipped step before depositing, and the single most-faked element of a scam platform.

The official registers

Where to verify a broker licence — the only sources that matter

  • ✓FCA Register (UK): register.fca.org.uk — search the exact company name, not the trading name.
  • ✓CySEC (Cyprus/EU): cysec.gov.cy — the dominant regulator for EU forex and CFD brokers.
  • ✓ASIC Connect (Australia): connectonline.asic.gov.au — search ABN and company name.
  • ✓SEC EDGAR + FINRA BrokerCheck (US): sec.gov/edgar and brokercheck.finra.org.
  • ✓CFTC / NFA (US derivatives): cftc.gov and nfa.futures.org.
The clone trick

How scam brokers fake a licence — and how to catch it

Clone firms copy the name, registration number and branding of a real, licensed firm. The licence is real — but it belongs to someone else. The catch: the clone's website URL, phone number and email domain differ from the licensed firm's official records by even one character.

The three-step check: (1) Find the licence number on the broker's site. (2) Search it on the official regulator's register — not via Google, directly. (3) Compare the registered website, phone and address on the regulator's record with the site you are actually on. If any detail differs, it is a clone.

Red flags

Licence claims that are always fake

  • ✓A licence from 'FINAEU', 'CARFIS', or another regulator that does not exist — the only real Canadian SRO is CIRO; the only real UK regulator is the FCA.
  • ✓A registration number that returns no result on the official register, or a result for a different company.
  • ✓A 'licence' that is only a PDF on the broker's own website, with no verifiable record on the regulator's public register.
  • ✓A regulator badge image that doesn't link to a live record on the regulator's website.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

How do I check if a broker is FCA regulated?

Go directly to register.fca.org.uk and search the exact company name — not the trading name, and not via Google. The FCA register shows the firm's authorised status, its registered website, and its approved activities. If the website you are on differs from the registered website, it is a clone firm.

Is a CySEC licence as good as an FCA licence?

No. CySEC is a real regulator but a lighter one — no meaningful compensation scheme and weaker escalation if the operator digs in. An FCA licence comes with FSCS protection up to £85,000 and a much stricter conduct regime. Treat CySEC-licensed brokers with more caution, and size your deposits accordingly.

What if the broker's licence number doesn't exist?

That is a definitive red flag. Either the broker is unregulated and lying, or it is a clone using a fabricated number. Do not deposit. Report the site to the FCA's clone-firm warning list and to Action Fraud.
