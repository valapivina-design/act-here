Chargeback Failed Investment? Bank, Court & Tracing Options

Your bank said no. Or the 120-day window closed while you were still hoping the broker would pay. A failed chargeback feels like the end of the road — but in UK investment-fraud litigation, it is often just the beginning.

Why chargebacks fail

The four reasons banks deny investment-fraud chargebacks

  • ✓Authorised push payment: you sent the money yourself, so the bank treats it as your choice — even if you were defrauded into sending it.
  • ✓The window closed: card chargebacks typically must be filed within 120 days of the transaction, and most victims wait far longer.
  • ✓'Services as described': the broker points to a trading dashboard and claims you received what you paid for — access to a platform.
  • ✓Crypto payments: chargebacks don't exist on-chain, and banks treat crypto purchases as final.
What still works

Three routes after a failed chargeback

Civil claim for fraud or breach of contract

A chargeback is a banking remedy. A civil claim is a legal one. If the broker made false promises, changed its terms after you paid, or denied a documented withdrawal, you have a claim in contract and tort — regardless of what the bank decided. UK county-court and High Court routes are available depending on amount.

Crypto tracing to the cash-out exchange

Even if you paid in crypto, the trail is not lost. Every transaction is on a public ledger, and the operator must eventually convert to fiat at an exchange. A forensic trace that identifies the cash-out exchange is what forces that exchange to freeze accounts and cooperate with law enforcement.

Multi-victim coordination

One victim is a dispute. Fifty victims is a fraud pattern. Act Here coordinates claims across multiple victims of the same platform, which changes the economics of litigation and the attention of regulators.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

My bank denied my chargeback. Do I have any other options?

Yes. A chargeback is only a banking remedy, not a legal one. You can still sue the broker for fraud or breach of contract, trace crypto payments to the cash-out exchange, and join a coordinated multi-victim claim. The bank's 'no' does not end the case.

How long do I have to sue after a failed investment?

In England and Wales, contract and tort claims typically have a 6-year limitation period from the date of the breach or the date you discovered the fraud. Evidence quality matters more than time — the sooner you preserve screenshots and communications, the stronger the claim.

Can I chargeback a crypto payment?

Not on-chain. But if you bought the crypto with a card specifically to send to the scammer, some banks will consider a chargeback on the card purchase itself. The stronger route is forensic tracing to the exchange where the operator cashes out.
